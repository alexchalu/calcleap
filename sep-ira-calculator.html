How this SEP-IRA calculator works

A SEP-IRA (Simplified Employee Pension) is authorized by IRC §408(k) and is the lowest-friction retirement plan for small businesses and self-employed individuals. Unlike a 401(k) or SIMPLE IRA, all contributions are employer contributions — there is no employee elective deferral and no age-50 catch-up. For sole proprietors, "employer" and "employee" are the same person, so a Schedule C filer contributes to their own SEP based on their business's net profit.

The 2026 numbers, from IRS Notice 2025-67 and IR-2025-176

  • §415(c) DC ceiling: $72,000 for 2026 (up from $70,000 in 2025). This is the absolute maximum from all sources for a single participant.
  • §401(a)(17) compensation cap: $360,000 for 2026 (up from $350,000 in 2025). Compensation above this amount is ignored when computing the SEP contribution.
  • Nominal contribution rate: up to 25% under IRC §404(h)(1)(C).
  • Effective rate for sole proprietors: 25%/(1+25%) = 20% per IRC §401(c)(2), applied to adjusted net earnings from self-employment.
  • Practical ceiling for a sole prop: $72,000/20% = $360,000 of adjusted net earnings. At exactly $360,000, both the §415(c) $72K ceiling and the §401(a)(17) $360K cap bind simultaneously.
  • Practical ceiling for W-2 employees: $72,000/25% = $288,000 of W-2 wages. Above $288,000, the §415(c) ceiling binds before the §401(a)(17) cap.

Why sole proprietors get 20% instead of 25%

The 25% rate under §404(h)(1)(C) is a percentage of "compensation." For a W-2 employee, compensation is a fixed number on the W-2. For a self-employed person, IRC §401(c)(2) defines compensation as net earnings from self-employment reduced by both one-half of self-employment tax (§164(f)) and the retirement plan contribution itself. Because compensation depends on the contribution, and the contribution depends on compensation, the algebra collapses to:

contribution = adjusted_net_earnings × rate / (1 + rate)

For rate = 25%, effective rate = 25%/1.25 = 20%. This is the standard IRS Publication 560 Rate Table result, and it's why every SEP-IRA guide and Solo 401(k) employer-contribution calculation for a sole prop uses 20% rather than 25%.

The exact worksheet the calculator uses for sole proprietors

  • Step 1: Net earnings from self-employment (§1402(a)) = Schedule C net profit × 0.9235 (this is the 92.35% factor per IRC §1402(a)(12) that excludes the employer-side half of SE tax before computing SE tax).
  • Step 2: SE tax = min(net_se_earnings, $184,500) × 12.4% (SS wage base 2026) + net_se_earnings × 2.9% (Medicare, uncapped). Additional Medicare Tax (0.9% above $200K/$250K) is not part of the SE tax deduction and is not modeled here — it does not affect the SEP contribution.
  • Step 3: Half-SE-tax deduction = SE_tax / 2 per §164(f).
  • Step 4: Adjusted net earnings = Schedule C net profit − half-SE-tax. Per Pub 560, this is the starting point for applying the rate.
  • Step 5: SEP contribution = min(effective_rate × min(adjusted_net_earnings, $360,000), $72,000) where effective_rate = rate/(1+rate).
